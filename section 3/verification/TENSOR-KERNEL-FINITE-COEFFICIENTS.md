# T001: finite degree-zero RES coefficients do not generate the kernel

The proposed restriction to finite X in RES[0] fails in the unlocalized,
graded tensor ring. Some positive-dimensional RES coefficients are
essential. This note constructs an explicit degree-two kernel element
outside the ideal generated by all finite-coefficient translation
relations. The separation is independent of the full kernel-generation
or unary-reduction theorems: membership of the new element is a direct
RV bijection, and non-membership is a balanced tensor detector.

This is a mathematical counterexample, not a completed concrete Lean
model or exported Lean invariant. No HK result is taken as a premise and
the approved manuscript is unchanged. It rules out the finite-only
family; it does not assert that every individual coefficient in the
larger family is indispensable or that a minimal family is known.

## The candidate finite ideal

Keep the usual graded ring

    T = K RES[*] tensor_(K Gamma^fin[*]) K Gamma[*].

Let J_fin be the ideal generated by

    [F]_0 tensor ([U]_m-[U+delta e_i]_m),                     (1)

for every finite S-definable RES[0] object F, every original-ground
definable function F -> delta^sharp, and all Gamma factors U. Allowing
arbitrary m here makes the proposed finite family as large as the
earlier full-coordinate presentation; the counterexample is not an
artifact of the unary simplification. In particular it also excludes
the ideal generated by just the finite-coefficient unary ray relations.

Finite RES objects assigned a positive label degree add no new
generators: a finite F in RES[r] is isomorphic to F in RES[0] times the
degree-r singleton with label (1,...,1). Thus its relation is already
a multiple of a relation in (1).

## A connected curve supplies a multiplier

Take S=C((t)), with val(t)=1, and delta=1/5. The old-ground definable RV
elements have integer values. Let x range over the residue field
k minus {0,1}, regarded inside the zero-value RV fibre, and define

    X = {(x,y) : x in k minus {0,1}, val(y)=1/5,
                 y^5=rv(t) x(x-1)}.                        (2)

Here x(x-1) is nonzero residue-field multiplication, canonically
identified with a value-zero RV element. There is no ambiguous RV
subtraction at a cancellation point: x=1 has been excluded.

Projection f(x,y)=x is finite, with five points in every fibre. The
source has the single value tuple (0,1/5), so (X,f) is an actual RES[1]
object. The function c(x,y)=y is an S-definable map X -> delta^sharp.

Write H_a=(a,infinity). Consequently

    z_X = [X]_1 tensor ([H_0]_1-[H_(1/5)]_1)                 (3)

lies in ker(Psi), by the original-ground RV bijection

    (x,y,u) |-> (x,y,yu),

whose inverse divides the last coordinate by y. Both product objects
have degree two, with finite labels (x,u) and (x,yu), respectively.

After choosing an RV fifth root beta of rv(t) only for examining the
residue geometry, write y=beta*z. The curve becomes

    z^5=x(x-1), x not in {0,1}, z not zero.                  (4)

It is irreducible: x(x-1) is not a fifth power in C(x). Its smooth
projective normalization has genus two. Indeed the degree-five map to
the x-line is totally ramified at 0, 1, and infinity; Riemann--Hurwitz
gives 2g-2=-10+3*4=2. The cyclic descent action is z |-> zeta*z for
zeta in mu_5. It preserves the single irreducible curve component
setwise. This component is not pointwise fixed; the action is free on
the open set (4).

## A low-degree RES invariant

We use only ordinary same-degree isomorphisms and scissors, with the
finite-label grading retained. Every RES[1] object, after trivializing
its finitely many value fibres by a common RV root of rv(t), becomes a
constructible set of dimension at most one over C, with a finite cyclic
descent action. Its finite label implies that dimension bound. Its
degree-zero counterpart is a finite set with such an action.

More explicitly, take a common denominator N for the finitely many
rational values of the source and its label, choose beta_N with
beta_N^N=rv(t), and divide a coordinate of value q by beta_N^(Nq).
The normalized coordinates have value zero. The induced structure on
these fibres is the residue-field structure, so the normalized sets
and graphs are constructible over C. Changing beta_N to zeta*beta_N
produces the diagonal finite cyclic action. This is explicit
fixed-ground descent bookkeeping, not an assumption of the HK tensor
comparison or a choice of a root over S.

For a finite RES[0] object F, put

    n(F) = number of S-definable points of F.

This is equivalently the number of points fixed by the entire cyclic
descent action. The equivalence uses the henselian ground with residue
field C: after the above normalization a finite constructible set has
coordinates algebraic over C, hence in C. An RV tuple fixed by all the
root rotations has integer values and belongs to RV(S).

For a RES[1] object Y, put

    b(Y) = number of geometric irreducible curve components
           of positive genus preserved setwise by the entire
           cyclic descent action.                         (5)

Finite components and genus-zero curves contribute zero. Genus means
that of the smooth projective normalization of the function field.
This construction uses only curves; it is not being asserted as a
ring invariant of the ungraded Grothendieck ring of all varieties.

The following checks establish the invariant we need.

* Enlarging N replaces the cyclic action by a surjective pullback, and
  changing the chosen root conjugates the normalized presentation.
  Neither operation changes the component orbits or their genera.
* A constructible bijection between curves in characteristic zero
  induces birational bijections between their dense open components.
  An original-ground definable bijection is equivariant for descent.
  Thus (5) is invariant under the actual RES[1] isomorphisms, including
  changes of finite label. It is additive under scissors within
  RES[1]: a curve component has a dense open in at most one part of a
  finite constructible partition. Finite parts do not affect b.
* A component of F x Y is a pair (p,C). It is preserved setwise by the
  whole descent group exactly when p is fixed and C is preserved.
  Hence b(F x Y)=n(F)b(Y). Also n(F x F')=n(F)n(F').
* A value fibre a^sharp is a rational multiplicative curve, whatever
  its cyclic rotation action. Therefore b(a^sharp)=0.

In particular b(X)=1 for (2). The grading is essential: no scissor
relation involving a dimension-two object is imposed on the
degree-one curve group. Promoting the same curve to label degree two
gives a distinct homogeneous class, which is treated in degree two
below.

## The Gamma invariant

Use the same unary boundary invariant as in the first finite-root
obstruction. Define A:Q -> Z by

    A(q)=1 on 1/5+Z, -1 on -1/5+Z, and 0 on the other cosets,
    A(-infinity)=A(infinity)=0.

It is odd and integer-periodic. On a unary interval with endpoints
a<b, set nu(interval)=A(a)-A(b), independently of endpoint inclusion;
points have value zero. Subdivision is additive by telescoping, and
reflection plus integer translation preserves nu. The actual
original-ground Gamma[1] morphisms are piecewise such maps in this
VF-generated henselian example; this also follows from the local
one-variable generic monomial calculation and old-ground RV
coefficients. Thus nu is a valid additive invariant of Gamma[1].
In particular

    nu(H_0)=0, nu(H_(1/5))=1,

and nu is invariant under every integer translation. Finite unary
Gamma objects have value zero.

## The balanced tensor detector

Let

    D=Z[eta,epsilon]/(eta^2,epsilon^2),
    degree eta=degree epsilon=1.

The product eta*epsilon is a nonzero degree-two element. Define maps
on the graded RES and Gamma rings by

| Source degree | RES map | Gamma map |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | [F] -> n(F) | [C] -> cardinality(C) |
| 1 | [Y] -> b(Y)*eta | [U] -> nu(U)*epsilon |
| at least 2 | 0 | 0 |

These are ring homomorphisms. Additivity and isomorphism invariance
were just checked. Degree-zero multiplication is finite product or
disjoint repetition, with b(F x Y)=n(F)b(Y). Products of positive
RES degrees map to eta^2=0; products of positive Gamma degrees map
to epsilon^2=0. All remaining products either have these forms or
are in a killed higher degree.

They agree on the entire finite-Gamma coefficient ring. In degree
zero its lift is a finite set of definable tags, so both maps give
cardinality. In degree one the RES lift is a finite union of rational
value fibres, with b=0, and the Gamma invariant nu is zero on the
finite set. All higher coefficient degrees map to zero on both sides.
The actual balanced tensor universal property therefore gives a ring
homomorphism

    Phi:T -> D.                                             (6)

No map from K RV to D is asserted; the detector is on the tensor
source, precisely to distinguish relations whose RV images vanish.

## All finite-coefficient generators vanish, but z_X does not

Suppose F is finite and c:F -> delta^sharp is original-ground
definable. If n(F)>0, evaluating c at an S-definable point gives an
S-definable RV element of value delta. Necessarily delta is an integer.
For such a delta, nu(U)=nu(U+delta) whenever U is unary. If n(F)=0,
the entire finite RES coefficient maps to zero. Thus every degree-one
generator (1) maps to zero. Generators with Gamma arity at least two
map to zero by the Gamma degree truncation. Consequently

    Phi(J_fin)=0.                                          (7)

On the other hand b(X)=1, so (3) gives

    Phi(z_X)=eta*(0-epsilon)=-eta*epsilon != 0.              (8)

Combining (3), (7) and (8) proves

    z_X in ker(Psi), z_X not in J_fin,
    J_fin strictly contained in ker(Psi).

This is an ideal-level separation allowing arbitrary sums, ring
multipliers, and additive group completion. It does not merely observe
that the finite cover X -> k minus {0,1} is not a product. Its varying
connected curve geometry gives an obstruction not captured by the
finite-coefficient ideal.

## Consequence and formalization boundary

The full coefficient family might admit other refinements, but finite
degree-zero coefficients alone cannot generate the graded kernel.
Already a RES[1] coefficient is needed. Finite coefficients placed
in higher degrees do not fix this, by the promotion observation after
(1). No statement about an ungraded variant, a unary positive
congruence, or a minimal positive-dimensional family follows here.

The Laurent-series setting, cyclic constructible descent, genus and
the detector still require complete local Lean constructions. The
mathematical separation is written explicitly here rather than
encoded as a new premise. No Lean source, verification receipt or
approved working manuscript was changed.
