# Hrushovski–Loeser Lemma 8.5.2 and its sources

Investigation of 2026-11-20, at the author's request. Sources read directly: the published
Hrushovski–Loeser paper (§8.4–8.5) and arXiv:math/0609115v3 of Hrushovski–Kazhdan, *The value ring of
geometric motivic integration, and the Iwahori Hecke algebra of SL₂* (§2). The published GAFA version of
the latter was not read.

## The statement

Δ ⊆ Γⁿ bounded and definable; ℓ : Δ → Γ piecewise of the form Σ aᵢxᵢ + b with aᵢ, b ∈ ℤ. Put

    Z(Δ, ℓ)(T) = Σ_{m ≥ 1}  Σ_{γ ∈ Δ ∩ (m⁻¹ℤ)ⁿ}  U^{−mℓ(γ)} T^m   ∈ ℤ[U, U⁻¹][[T]].

Then Z(Δ, ℓ) ∈ ℤ[U, U⁻¹][T]† and lim_{T→∞} Z(Δ, ℓ) = −χ(Δ).

## How Hrushovski–Loeser prove it

1. The property "the lemma holds for Δ and every ℓ" depends only on the class of Δ in the dimension-free
   semiring K₊^df(Γ^bdd_ℤ) (morphisms: piecewise GLₙ(ℤ) with translations in ℤⁿ, which preserve
   (m⁻¹ℤ)ⁿ).
2. Intervals with rational endpoints and points: a geometric series.
3. Products of these: the Hadamard product, Lemma 8.4.1, whose proof is "Propositions 5.1.1 and 5.1.2 of
   Denef–Loeser (Thom–Sebastiani paper) and their proofs".
4. Every class a satisfies m·a + b = c with b, c in the sub-semiring K′ generated by the classes of points
   and of intervals [0, γ): "Lemma 2.21 of [20]".

## The Hrushovski–Kazhdan result

In arXiv v3 the numbering is shifted by one: the generation statement is **Proposition 2.21** and the
statement used by Hrushovski–Loeser is the second sentence of **Lemma 2.22**:

> for any a ∈ K₊₀(Γ) there exist m ∈ ℕ and b, c ∈ K₊₀(Γ)′ such that ma + b = c in K₊₀(Γ).

What it rests on (all in §2 of that paper; no outside result is cited in the chain):

| Step | Content |
| --- | --- |
| §2.3 | rings of definable functions Γ → K^df(Γ^bdd_{A(t)}), with the base group varying |
| §2.5, Lemma 2.6 | dimension filtration Fₙ and the graded integral |
| Lemma 2.7, Cor. 2.8, Lemma 2.10 | integration by parts, valid modulo lower filtration ("=_gr") |
| Claim 2.9, Cor. 2.11 | ∫ι(t)ⁿ = ι(b)ⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) in the graded ring |
| Lemmas 2.13, 2.14 | normal form for products of the idempotents e(αx + βb) ("divisibility terms") |
| Lemmas 2.17, 2.18 | one-dimensional sets: segments, moved to a coordinate axis by GLₙ(ℤ) |
| Lemma 2.19 | ∫ of Π ι(αᵢt + cᵢ) by induction on the common denominator d |
| Lemma 2.20 | ∫₀ˣ f for f ∈ 𝓕ₙ; uses compactness over all extensions A(b) |
| Prop. 2.21 | K^df_ℚ(Γ^bdd_A) is generated as a ℚ-algebra by e(a), ι(a); induction on dimension |
| Lemma 2.22 | the semiring refinement; the proof is five lines |

Why this is not easy to prove in full:

- The chain works in K^df_ℚ, a ring tensored with ℚ, and the integration-by-parts identities hold only
  modulo lower filtration. The statement Hrushovski–Loeser need is in the *semiring* K₊₀. Its proof reads
  "All our integral equalities are valid in K′_ℚ. Hence the proof of Proposition 2.21 shows that
  K′_ℚ → K″_ℚ is surjective". Making this precise means redoing every step of the chain at the level of
  explicit piecewise GLₙ(ℤ)-bijections, with the lower-order terms carried along.
- The function rings are defined by evaluating at all one-generated extensions A(t) of the base group and
  use a compactness argument (Lemma 2.20) to pass from pointwise to uniform identities.
- In Lean none of the objects exist: no category Γ^bdd[n] with GLₙ(ℤ)-morphisms as a graded semiring, no
  dimension-free localisation, no function rings over varying bases.

## A gap in the deduction (my reading)

Step 3 covers a box with a weight that is affine on the whole box: then ℓ splits as a sum over the
factors and the series is a Hadamard product. But the property in step 1 is "for every piecewise
affine ℓ", and the pieces of a piecewise affine weight on a box are not boxes. Applying step 4 to a piece
returns a union of boxes carrying the transported weight, which is again only piecewise affine, so the
argument is circular. Even for a weight that is affine on Δ, the weight transported to the boxes of c is
only piecewise affine. As far as I can see, what is needed is a version of Lemma 2.22 for pairs
(Δ, ℓ), which is closer to the measured categories of §2.11–§3 of Hrushovski–Kazhdan than to Lemma 2.22
itself. So a complete proof of Lemma 2.22 would still not complete this route.

## A direct proof (recommended)

Identify the definable value of t with 1, so that Δ is a bounded subset of ℚⁿ given by a Boolean
combination of ℚ-affine inequalities, and (m⁻¹ℤ)ⁿ is the usual lattice.

**Step 1 (triangulation).** Δ is a finite disjoint union of open simplices with rational vertices, and the
simplices can be chosen so that ℓ is affine on each. Proof by induction on n: solve each inequality for
the last coordinate, y ⋚ φᵢ(x); triangulate the bounded projection compatibly with the signs of all
φᵢ − φⱼ and with the inequalities not involving y. Over an open simplex σ with vertices v₀, …, v_k the
φᵢ are ordered, so the set above σ is a union of graphs and of bands f < y < g. A graph is an open
k-simplex. For a band put hⱼ(vᵢ) = f(vᵢ) for i ≤ j and g(vᵢ) for i > j (so h₋₁ = g, h_k = f). The hⱼ
decrease, hⱼ − hⱼ₊₁ vanishes at every vertex but vⱼ₊₁, and the band is the disjoint union of the graphs
of the intermediate hⱼ and the bands hⱼ₊₁ < y < hⱼ, each of which is an open (k+1)-simplex.

**Step 2 (one simplex).** Let σ be an open simplex with vertices w₀, …, w_k and ℓ = a·x + b on σ. Put
vⱼ = mⱼ·(wⱼ, 1) ∈ ℤⁿ⁺¹ with mⱼ ≥ 1 the least denominator, and L(x, m) = a·x + bm. The lattice points
(x, m) with m ≥ 1 and x/m ∈ σ are the lattice points of the open cone on the vⱼ, and each is uniquely
p + Σ nⱼvⱼ with nⱼ ∈ ℕ and p in the finite set Π of lattice points Σ μⱼvⱼ, 0 < μⱼ ≤ 1. Hence

    Z(σ, ℓ)(T) = ( Σ_{p ∈ Π} U^{−L(p)} T^{m(p)} ) / Π_j (1 − U^{−L(vⱼ)} T^{mⱼ}).

The denominator is of the allowed form (mⱼ ≥ 1). The T-degree m(p) = Σ μⱼmⱼ is at most Σ mⱼ, with
equality only for p = Σ vⱼ, so the degree is ≤ 0 and the limit is
U^{−L(Σvⱼ)} / ((−1)^{k+1} U^{−L(Σvⱼ)}) = (−1)^{k+1} = −χ(σ).

**Step 3.** Z and χ are additive over the finite disjoint union, and lim is additive on series of
degree ≤ 0.

This uses neither Lemma 8.4.1 (Hadamard product) nor anything from Hrushovski–Kazhdan, and handles
piecewise weights directly. Step 2 was checked numerically (25 random rational simplices in dimensions
1 and 2 with random integral weights: the series agrees with the closed formula up to T¹⁴, and the
numerator has exactly one term of top degree); the script was not kept.

The formal statement over ℚ: `D` is a bounded union of sign classes of finitely many affine functions;
the weight is `a_r·x + b_r m` (integral) on each member of a cover of `D` by unions of sign classes;
then the series has a limit in the sense of `HasLimit`, equal to `−χ(D)`, where `χ(D)` is the alternating
sum over any triangulation. Independence of the triangulation is proved from the uniqueness of the limit
(weight 0 over ℤ). No `sorry`; axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.

## Consequence for the manuscript

The author decided on 2026-11-20 that the paper carries the direct proof in place of the citation. It is
now Lemma 8.9 (label `lat:zeta`) of the manuscript, with a preceding paragraph that defines R[T]†, the
degree and the limit for a general ring R and unit A (batch B07b, applied 2026-11-20). The proof of
Theorem 8.11 (batch B08) applies that lemma to the ring K^μ̂ Var_ℂ[[𝔸]⁻¹] and does not use the Hadamard
product.

## Use in Theorem 8.11 (Lean)

The proof of Theorem 8.11 in Lean (`Theorem811.lean`) does not decompose the weight into integral
affine pieces. For a product generator `[g♯ × V]` it applies the lemma to the graph of
`δ ↦ (w σ(δ), c)` over the support of `g`, with the integral linear weight `−(first graph
coordinate)`: the lattice points of the graph are exactly the lattice points `δ` at which the
exponent `−m(σ(δ) + w)` is integral and the value tuple `c` of the RES piece is a lattice point.
`χ(graph) = χ(support)` by `integrateAll_bijOn`.
