# Section 7 gap and convention record

Independently checked against the revised main text before the referee notes.
None of these is a mathematical error; each is an omitted justification or a
formalization convention. No manuscript edit has been made for them.

1. **Grothendieck semigroups and isomorphism.** Definition 3.1 presents
   K₊ by scissor relations. 7.8, 7.10, 7.24 and 7.25 use that equal classes
   come from isomorphic objects. For μRV^db[k] the Lean class monoid
   `BoundedClasses.Cls` *is* the set of isomorphism classes under ⊎. The
   scissor relation holds there (`cls_partition`), so the two presentations
   agree. The same fact for K₊ μVF^◇[k] is still to be proved (needed in 7.24).
2. **Blowups on classes.** The paper remarks after Corollary 7.8 that the blowup
   relations may be regarded on isomorphism classes. The Lean definitions
   (`ElementaryStep`, `Step`, `BlowupSteps`, `MuIsp`) are stated on classes. A
   length-one blowup replaces summands `[U_i]` by `[U_i^♭]`, which is the class
   form of `(V ∖ C) ⊎ ⊎ U_i^♭` via `F_i : U_i ≅ C_i`. Still to do: the
   object-level converse, that a class-level blowup is realised by literal
   disjoint subobjects of a given `V`.
3. **∞ as a tag.** As in Section 4, objects are bounded charts whose label
   coordinates may equal ∞, and the coproduct tags with ∞. Section 4's
   `RVStrictBoundedComparison` shows this presentation and the paper's ∞-free
   one give isomorphic rings. The lift to μVF^◇ (milestone M3) needs ∞-free
   labels and will convert there.
4. **Named aperture bound.** Section 4's `Aperture.bound` is a Γ value without a
   definability requirement. For doubly bounded objects a named bound exists by
   Remark 7.4 (`Remark74.remark_7_4_named`).
5. **Lemma 7.23.** "By compactness" is not needed: restricting the first two
   stages of a standard contraction to the fibres over `A_E` already gives the
   fibrewise standard contractions.
6. **Lemma 7.5.** The proof splits by the pointwise order of the depths and
   uses the ratio aperture. As formalized, its comparison maps are explicit
   definable bijections, and its multipliers are coordinates or values of
   definable functions. Equal depths with different endpoints are covered:
   the second blowup has depth zero and an endpoint of valuation zero.
7. **Lemma 7.12, route and form.** The proof's map Φ is formalized as Section 6
   relative aperture data on the lift (focus b₀, endpoint b₁), which already
   carries the two branch tags. The label `s = rv(x − b₀)/f_j(u)` of the shell
   branch is recorded by Section 6's regularization (all leading terms become
   labels) rather than by a separate label. The regularized image is the RV
   pullback of explicit endpoint and shell labels. Section 6's contraction and
   Remark 6.15 give `𝕃U ≅ 𝕃W` in μVF^◇. The contraction `W` splits by tag
   into pieces isomorphic in μRV^db to the shell and the endpoint. So the Lean
   statement is the equality `μ𝕃[U^♭] = μ𝕃[U]` in K₊ μVF^◇ rather than an
   isomorphism of objects. The later uses (the "if" direction of 7.24, and the
   generators of J_var in the kernel in 7.26) need only this class form. Labels
   equal to ∞ are handled by the strict chart: Lemma 7.5's comparison
   isomorphisms for `strictIso` have equal depths, so the strict datum has the
   same source and blowup classes.
8. **Lemma 7.13, hypothesis.** The proof applies Lemma 6.38 to a morphism
   𝕃U → 𝕃V, so it uses an isomorphism A ≅ B. The Lean hypothesis is that
   isomorphism (`KernelDimOne.lemma_7_13`). Equality in the scissor-presented
   K₊ μVF^◇[1] gives it once item 1 is proved for μVF^◇. Alternatively, 7.24
   can pass from K₊ to μIsp^db directly: 7.13 makes the class of a standard
   contraction independent of the choice and invariant under isomorphism, and
   restriction to pieces is additive. The "standard contraction" is Section 6's
   `NormalizedMeasuredContraction` with head start 0, whose lift is
   isomorphic to the object by Remark 6.28.
9. **Adjoining VF parameters (S⟨a⟩).** 7.14 and 7.15 work over `S⟨a⟩`. In the
   formal one-sorted language the residue addition is constrained only at
   residue units. So the substructure generated by `S` and `a` need not have
   all its RV elements of the form rv(c), with c in its VF part. It then has no
   Section 2 `VFGeneratedFieldBase`, which Section 6 states Lemma 6.38 over.
   This is a feature of the formal language, not of the paper. The proof of
   6.38 uses only support data V ⊆ S ⊆ acl(V), except for a cited ordinary
   matching, which has a without-field upstream version. `SetBaseMatching`
   restates 6.38 over support data, and `KernelBaseChange.adjoin` with
   `adjoin_support` supplies such data for `S⟨a⟩`. No manuscript change is
   needed.
10. **Lemma 7.14, formal reading.** `[(A'_a, ω')]_{≤k}` is read through
    head-start labels π (degree-k volume u ↦ u ∘ π), as in Section 6's head
    starts and Remark 6.28; the selected labels are finite-to-one and nonzero
    on the fibres. In degree 0, μIsp^db is equality of classes (there are no
    blowups). The base is a substructure with support data (item 9).
11. **Corollary 7.15 and the preliminary normalization (found 2026-11-19;
    resolved for 7.15 the same day, without changing the 7.14 input).** A standard contraction first adjusts the set by Remark
    `spec:addendum`, so that it is doubly bounded ("Adjusting T_i by way of
    Remark spec:addendum if necessary"). In Section 6 this is the zero-centred
    normalization of `NormalizedMeasuredContraction`: every coordinate smaller
    than a definable center c is shifted by c. The two standard contractions in
    7.15 may use different centers. Their first-stage sets T̂_{σ(1)}(A') and
    R̂_{σ(1)}(A'') then have VF projections shift_c'(P) and shift_c''(P) of the
    common base P = pr_{~i}(A') = pr_{~i}(A''). These differ, while Lemma 7.14
    needs a common VF projection. The proof of 7.15, and R126's "common base",
    do not address this.
    - It is an omitted justification, not a mathematical error. The shifts are
      coordinatewise piecewise translations by base-definable constants. The
      centre is only definable over the base (`ZeroCenteredData.center_definable`)
      and need not lie in the substructure `T`, so `adjoin T a` and
      `adjoin T (shift a)` need not be equal. They have the same definable
      closure, so classes pass between them by `BaseExtension.Transfer.ofDcl`
      (correction of 2026-11-19, pointed out by the 7.14 session).
    - A repair is needed before 7.15 can be applied to arbitrary standard
      contractions. Options: (a) a form of 7.14 that allows the two VF
      projections to differ by such a coordinatewise translation; (b) a lemma
      that the class of a standard contraction does not depend on the
      normalization; (c) normalization-free contractions in 7.14/7.15 for
      doubly bounded objects, with (b) for the comparison.
    - Resolution for 7.15 (`Corollary715`, `ContractionChoice`). Option (b)
      follows from the 7.14 input itself: with A' = A'' the fibres coincide,
      so any two standard contractions of one object in the same order are
      μIsp^db-related (`ispRel_same_set`). It then suffices to treat
      contractions with a common centre (`exists_common_center`,
      `exists_contraction_with`), whose first-stage sets have the same VF
      projection (`firstSet_vf_iff`). Normalized contractions of the
      first-stage sets are grafted after the first stage (`graftRest`,
      `cls_graftRest`): a first-stage set is doubly bounded, so its
      normalization fixes the VF coordinates (Section 6
      `point_field_of_doublyBounded`) and the program pulls back
      (`precomposeFixedVF`).
    - Inside the proof of 7.14, where (b) is not yet available, the 7.14
      session uses option (a) internally. 7.23 and 7.24 can follow the 7.15
      route.

12. **Corollary 7.15: good points and invariance (formal reading,
    2026-11-19).** Omitted justifications, not errors.
    - The paper's grid lies in VF(S), with S closed under definable closure
      (Convention 2.5). Here the base is a substructure, so the grid points are
      powers of one element definable over S (`exists_good_point`), and the
      classes over S⟨b⟩ are carried to S⟨a⟩ through the definable closure
      (`transfer_adjoin`, `BaseExtension.Transfer.ofDcl`).
    - "δ-invariance" is needed for the fibres and for their forms. One named
      radius serves both (`FibreInvariance.firstSet_uniform`): Section 6's
      uniform radius applied to the graft of the rest-lift map, whose RV labels
      determine the form. The fibre classes at a and b then agree
      (`FibreComparison.cls_fibreObject_eq`).
    - "Bad parameters lie in Z(P)": for each bad set (the exceptional sets of
      the two first stages, and the image of the bad set of F in the prepared
      set), P is the leading coefficient in the contracted coordinate of a
      nonzero polynomial vanishing on it. Off Z(P) its slice is finite
      (`FibreRestriction.exists_good_polynomial`), and the slice maps are
      morphisms of μVF^◇[1] over S⟨b⟩ (`FibreRestriction.sliceMorphism`).

13. **Lemma 7.20, formal reading of the input (2026-11-19).** The input
    `TwoCellInput.Lemma720` states the paper's lemma as follows. These are
    readings, not changes of statement.
    - Retained labels: A ⊆ VF² × RVʳ. Lemma 7.22 applies 7.20 to the graph of θ
      with its RV coordinates retained ("its proof is uniform in such labels");
      r = 0 is the printed statement. Section 6's standard contractions retain
      the old labels, so each fibre lies over one label value.
    - Normalized coordinates: the fibre over s is read in the normalized
      coordinates of the contraction, the preliminary adjustment by Remark
      `spec:addendum` that a standard contraction may make (amended the same
      day; found by the 7.20 session). Read in the original coordinates, the
      statement is false: for A = 𝒪 × 𝒪 every zero-centred normalization moves 0
      (since v(0) = ∞ > v(c)), so the fibre through (0, 0) is an open set around
      (0, 0) and meets several RV-discs. On a fibre the core bits are tags in s,
      so the normalized fibre is the original one translated by a constant
      vector definable over T; for doubly bounded A the readings agree
      (Section 6 `point_field_of_doublyBounded`).
    - Orientation: the fibre over s is read in the order (x_{e 0}, x_{e 1}),
      where e is the coordinate order of the contraction. Its first stage
      contracts x_{e 1}, which therefore carries the RV-discs t^♯ + ε(a) of
      Definition 7.19. The paper leaves σ and the orientation implicit; σ is
      existential, as printed.
    - Uniform cell data: the input gives the positioning function ε(s, ·), the
      paradigm t(s), the second paradigm t₁(s) of a balanced cell and the kind of
      each cell as functions of s, with graphs and kind pieces definable over T
      (`UniformTwoCells`). Definition 7.17 asks the set and the positioning
      bijection to be definable, and Lemma 7.22 relies on 7.20's proof being
      "uniform in such labels": it applies Lemma 6.38 once to the family of
      positioning functions with s as a retained label. A positioning function
      is only determined up to a ball, so this uniformity cannot be recovered
      from fibrewise data without definable choice; it is part of the statement
      (amended the same day, before the 7.20 session began proving it).

14. **Lemma 7.22, formal reading (2026-11-19).** Omitted justifications and a
    formalization route, not errors.
    - The preparation of ω (R133): Section 6's measured preparation gives
      proper covariant RV data through which ω factors. Grafting them is a
      morphism fixing every VF coordinate, and the grafted form depends only on
      the labels (`Lemma722Objects.prepObject`).
    - The cells of Lemma 7.20 live in the normalized coordinates of its
      standard contraction (item 13). So `A*` is built on the normalized image
      `Y = D(A')`, by grafting the RV point of the contraction
      (`Lemma722Cells.cellObject`); its label fibres are exactly the cells
      (`cellOf_eq`). Built on `A'` itself, the label fibres would be the cells
      translated by the core shifts, and a zero-centred normalization of `A*`
      with the same centre need not exist: the cells need not be invariant at
      the radius of the centre.
    - Hence the printed "morphism `A → A*`, relatively unary in both
      coordinates" becomes a chain: the graft `A → A'`, the normalization
      `A' → Y`, the graft `Y → A*`. The two grafts fix every VF coordinate, so
      Corollary 7.15 applies to them in both orders. For the normalization, every
      contraction of `Y` pulls back to a contraction of `A'` with normalization `D`
      and the same class (`NormalizationComparison.cls_normalizedPullback`).
      Lemma 7.23 uses only the classes, so its conclusion is unchanged
      (`Lemma723Two.chain_order`).
    - "Compactness" is a finite gluing over the cell kinds. The kind is a
      function of the labels of `A*`, so the kinds form label pieces. Every
      contraction of `A*` restricts to each piece, with additive classes
      (`RestrictContractions.cls_headStart_zero_split`). On each piece any two
      contractions in one order are related by the independence of the centre
      (the 7.14 input) (`PieceCombination.ispRel_orders_of_pieces`).
    - Kinds 0 and 1 are products of two open discs (`ProductKinds.product_of_kind`).
      In the balanced kind the shift is automatically genuine: a balanced
      positioning function maps the first projection onto the second, which lies
      in one RV-disc (`BalancedKinds.preparedInput`).

15. **Proposition 7.24, the proof of (∗) (formal route, 2026-11-20).** Omitted
    justifications, not errors.
    - "We may assume that the two contractions have the same first stage": for
      orders `σ` and `σ ∘ s`, with `s` fixing the first contracted position, the
      common first stage is the conjugate of the first stage of `σ` by `s`
      (`StageReindexing.reindexStage`), grafted in the order `σ ∘ s`
      (`OrderGrafts.graftOrd`). Both classes become head-start-1 classes of
      contractions of one first-stage set, in the orders `1` and `s'`.
    - The fibre `T_h(B)_a` is the label slice of the first-stage set at
      `rv(a)` (`FibreContractions.mem_sliceAt`). Its form is covariant
      (`FirstStageObjects.firstForm_covariant`), but the whole first-stage set is
      not an object of `μVF^◇[k − 1]` (finite RV fibres only modulo the leading
      label); the label slices are.
    - The restriction of the remaining stages to the fibre is a base change to
      `S⟨a⟩` (`StageBaseChange.extendContraction`) followed by restriction to a
      label-saturated piece (`RestrictContractions.restrictContraction`).
    - The glued sets `⋃_a a × U_a` are the RV-disc bundles over the RV targets
      along the label carrying the leading label; their tautological
      contractions (`TautologicalContractions.taut`) have the classes of the two
      contractions (`cls_taut`).

16. **Proposition 7.24, the arrows of an isomorphism (input, 2026-11-20).** The
    proof applies Corollary 7.15 to "a sequence of relatively unary
    `μVF^◇[k]`-morphisms" `μL U = B_1 → … → B_{l+1} = μL V`. In the formal
    category (Section 6, approved R102), a morphism carries a factorization of
    its underlying map into relatively unary proper covariant homeomorphisms
    between definable sets, and the measure identity only for the composite;
    the intermediate sets carry no forms (Section 6 GAP-REPAIRS, design note to
    Definition 6.34). To apply Corollary 7.15 to each factor, the intermediate
    sets need covariant forms for which every factor satisfies the measure
    identity. With the forms transported from one end, this amounts to: the
    valuation of the Jacobian of a relatively unary proper covariant
    homeomorphism agrees, off a set of smaller VF-dimension, with a function
    constant on boxes of a named radius (a one-variable Jacobian property on the
    fibres).
    - Author's decision (2026-11-20): "Input + parallel build", as for 7.20. The
      input is `IntermediateFormsInput.IntermediateForms M G`
      (`section 7/IntermediateFormsInput.lean`), stated for exactly the data of
      one `UnaryFactorization.step`: for an object `A` and a relatively unary
      proper covariant homeomorphism `f : A → B` onto a definable set, `B` has
      finite RV fibres and a covariant form for which `f` has the measure
      identity at VF-generic points (`StepForms`). Section 6 supplies proper
      invariance of `B` and the morphism (`morphism_of_generic_map`).
    - Only `section 7/UnaryArrows.lean` unpacks it (`stepObject`,
      `stepMorphism`); the theorems using it take `(hIF : IntermediateForms M G)`.
