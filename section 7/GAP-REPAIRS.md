# Section 7 gap and convention record

Independently checked against the revised main text before the referee notes.
None of these is a mathematical error; each is an omitted justification or a
formalization convention. No manuscript edit has been made for them.

1. **Grothendieck semigroups and isomorphism.** Definition 3.1 presents
   K₊ by scissor relations. 7.8, 7.10, 7.24 and 7.25 use that equal classes
   come from isomorphic objects. For μRV^db[k] the Lean class monoid
   `BoundedClasses.Cls` *is* the set of isomorphism classes under ⊎. The
   scissor relation holds there (`cls_partition`), so the two presentations
   agree. The same fact for K₊ μVF^◇[k] is still to be proved (needed in 7.24).
2. **Blowups on classes.** The paper remarks after Corollary 7.8 that the blowup
   relations may be regarded on isomorphism classes. The Lean definitions
   (`ElementaryStep`, `Step`, `BlowupSteps`, `MuIsp`) are stated on classes. A
   length-one blowup replaces summands `[U_i]` by `[U_i^♭]`, which is the class
   form of `(V ∖ C) ⊎ ⊎ U_i^♭` via `F_i : U_i ≅ C_i`. Still to do: the
   object-level converse, that a class-level blowup is realised by literal
   disjoint subobjects of a given `V`.
3. **∞ as a tag.** As in Section 4, objects are bounded charts whose label
   coordinates may equal ∞, and the coproduct tags with ∞. Section 4's
   `RVStrictBoundedComparison` shows this presentation and the paper's ∞-free
   one give isomorphic rings. The lift to μVF^◇ (milestone M3) needs ∞-free
   labels and will convert there.
4. **Named aperture bound.** Section 4's `Aperture.bound` is a Γ value without a
   definability requirement. For doubly bounded objects a named bound exists by
   Remark 7.4 (`Remark74.remark_7_4_named`).
5. **Lemma 7.23.** "By compactness" is not needed: restricting the first two
   stages of a standard contraction to the fibres over `A_E` already gives the
   fibrewise standard contractions.
6. **Lemma 7.5.** The proof splits by the pointwise order of the depths and
   uses the ratio aperture. As formalized, its comparison maps are explicit
   definable bijections, and its multipliers are coordinates or values of
   definable functions. Equal depths with different endpoints are covered:
   the second blowup has depth zero and an endpoint of valuation zero.
7. **Lemma 7.12, route and form.** The proof's map Φ is formalized as Section 6
   relative aperture data on the lift (focus b₀, endpoint b₁), which already
   carries the two branch tags. The label `s = rv(x − b₀)/f_j(u)` of the shell
   branch is recorded by Section 6's regularization (all leading terms become
   labels) rather than by a separate label. The regularized image is the RV
   pullback of explicit endpoint and shell labels. Section 6's contraction and
   Remark 6.15 give `𝕃U ≅ 𝕃W` in μVF^◇. The contraction `W` splits by tag
   into pieces isomorphic in μRV^db to the shell and the endpoint. So the Lean
   statement is the equality `μ𝕃[U^♭] = μ𝕃[U]` in K₊ μVF^◇ rather than an
   isomorphism of objects. The later uses (the "if" direction of 7.24, and the
   generators of J_var in the kernel in 7.26) need only this class form. Labels
   equal to ∞ are handled by the strict chart: Lemma 7.5's comparison
   isomorphisms for `strictIso` have equal depths, so the strict datum has the
   same source and blowup classes.
8. **Lemma 7.13, hypothesis.** The proof applies Lemma 6.38 to a morphism
   𝕃U → 𝕃V, so it uses an isomorphism A ≅ B. The Lean hypothesis is that
   isomorphism (`KernelDimOne.lemma_7_13`). Equality in the scissor-presented
   K₊ μVF^◇[1] gives it once item 1 is proved for μVF^◇. Alternatively, 7.24
   can pass from K₊ to μIsp^db directly: 7.13 makes the class of a standard
   contraction independent of the choice and invariant under isomorphism, and
   restriction to pieces is additive. The "standard contraction" is Section 6's
   `NormalizedMeasuredContraction` with head start 0, whose lift is
   isomorphic to the object by Remark 6.28.
9. **Adjoining VF parameters (S⟨a⟩).** 7.14 and 7.15 work over `S⟨a⟩`. In the
   formal one-sorted language the residue addition is constrained only at
   residue units. So the substructure generated by `S` and `a` need not have
   all its RV elements of the form rv(c), with c in its VF part. It then has no
   Section 2 `VFGeneratedFieldBase`, which Section 6 states Lemma 6.38 over.
   This is a feature of the formal language, not of the paper. The proof of
   6.38 uses only support data V ⊆ S ⊆ acl(V), except for a cited ordinary
   matching, which has a without-field upstream version. `SetBaseMatching`
   restates 6.38 over support data, and `KernelBaseChange.adjoin` with
   `adjoin_support` supplies such data for `S⟨a⟩`. No manuscript change is
   needed.
